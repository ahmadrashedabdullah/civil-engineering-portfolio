GIS Flood Risk Assessment

Harris County, Texas

This project used ArcGIS Pro to assess flood risk across Harris County, Texas by integrating elevation, hydrological, and regulatory flood data. The analysis identified areas susceptible to flooding and compared GIS-derived flood-risk classifications with FEMA-designated flood zones.

ArcGIS Pro GIS Spatial Analysis DEM LiDAR FEMA NFHL Flood Risk

01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project Objective

The objective of this project was to analyze current flood risk in Harris County using GIS, compare FEMA flood maps with updated elevation data, identify areas where flood risk may be underestimated, and produce maps that clearly represent high-risk flood zones.

Using spatial data to identify flood risk that may not be fully represented by existing regulatory flood maps.

02 — PROBLEM STATEMENT

Why Flood Risk Matters

Harris County faces significant flood risk due to low-lying terrain, land subsidence, extreme weather, rapid urbanization, and its proximity to the Gulf of Mexico.

Existing regulatory flood maps may not always represent current environmental conditions. Updated spatial data can therefore provide additional information for flood-risk assessment, infrastructure planning, and disaster preparedness.

03 — STUDY AREA

Harris County, Texas

Harris County is located in southeastern Texas and contains the Houston metropolitan area. The county covers approximately 1,777 square miles and contains several major watersheds and drainage systems.

Area

Approximately 1,777 square miles.

Population

More than 4.7 million residents.

Major Watersheds

Armand Bayou, Clear Creek, and Sims Bayou.

04 — DATA USED

Spatial Datasets

Multiple spatial datasets were integrated to represent regulatory flood zones, ground elevation, and hydrological conditions across Harris County.

FEMA NFHL

Regulatory flood-zone information from the FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer.

DEM

Digital Elevation Model data representing ground elevation throughout the study area.

LiDAR

High-resolution elevation data used to improve understanding of low-lying terrain.

Watersheds

Watershed boundaries used to represent hydrological influences on flood risk.

Stream Networks

Drainage and stream information used to evaluate proximity to water bodies.

HCFCD Data

Spatial information from the Harris County Flood Control District.

06 — ELEVATION ANALYSIS

Identifying Low-Lying Areas

Digital Elevation Model data was used to represent ground elevation across Harris County.

Low-elevation areas were important because reduced elevation and limited drainage capacity can increase susceptibility to flooding.

07 — RASTER ANALYSIS

Raster Calculator

Raster-based analysis tools in ArcGIS Pro were used to evaluate elevation values and identify areas that may be more susceptible to flooding.

Watershed and stream-network information was incorporated to account for the increased flood susceptibility of areas near drainage channels and water bodies.

08 — FLOOD RISK CLASSIFICATION

Low, Moderate & High Risk

The Reclassify tool was used to organize elevation information into three flood-risk categories.

Low Risk

Generally associated with higher-elevation areas and improved natural drainage.

Moderate Risk

Transitional areas influenced by both elevation and proximity to waterways.

High Risk

Primarily low-elevation areas and locations near major watershed networks.

09 — FEMA COMPARISON

Overlay Analysis

The GIS-generated flood-risk classifications were compared with FEMA flood-hazard layers using spatial overlay analysis.

This comparison was used to identify locations where GIS-derived moderate or high flood risk extended beyond existing FEMA-designated flood zones.

10 — RESULTS

Key Findings

✓ High-risk areas were concentrated in low-elevation regions.

✓ Areas near major streams and watershed networks showed greater flood susceptibility.

✓ Moderate-risk areas occurred throughout transitional portions of the county.

✓ Higher-elevation areas generally showed lower flood susceptibility.

✓ Some GIS-derived moderate and high-risk areas extended beyond FEMA-designated flood zones.

Updated spatial analysis indicated that existing regulatory flood zones may underestimate flood risk in some locations.

11 — ENGINEERING APPLICATION

Planning & Infrastructure

GIS-based flood-risk assessment can support civil engineering decisions related to infrastructure, land-use planning, stormwater management, disaster preparedness, and community resilience.

Identifying areas of underestimated flood risk can help planners and engineers make better-informed decisions about future development and mitigation strategies.

12 — LIMITATIONS

Analysis Limitations

The analysis primarily used elevation and proximity to water bodies and did not include detailed hydrodynamic modeling or real-time rainfall data.

Dataset resolution and accuracy may also affect results, and future climate-change impacts such as increased rainfall intensity and sea-level rise were not directly modeled.

13 — CONCLUSION

Project Conclusion

This project demonstrated how ArcGIS Pro and updated spatial datasets can be used to improve understanding of flood risk across Harris County.

Integrating elevation, hydrological, and regulatory flood information revealed spatial patterns that may not be fully represented by existing floodplain maps.

Modern GIS tools can support more informed infrastructure planning, flood mitigation, and disaster preparedness.
