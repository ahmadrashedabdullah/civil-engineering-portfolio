FORENSIC & EARTHQUAKE ENGINEERING

I-5/SR-14 Earthquake Failure Investigation

Forensic Engineering Analysis of the 1971 San Fernando & 1994 Northridge Interchange Failures

This project investigates the repeated earthquake-related failures of the I-5/SR-14 Newhall Pass Interchange in Southern California. The investigation compares structural damage, possible failure mechanisms, seismic vulnerabilities, reconstruction measures, and engineering lessons associated with the 1971 San Fernando and 1994 Northridge earthquakes.

Forensic Engineering Bridge Engineering Earthquake Engineering Failure Analysis Seismic Retrofit

01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project Objective

The objective of this project was to investigate and compare the causes, structural damage, and failure sequences associated with the I-5/SR-14 interchange collapses during the 1971 San Fernando earthquake and the 1994 Northridge earthquake.

The investigation focused on understanding why portions of the interchange failed during both earthquakes and what engineers can learn from the two events to improve seismic bridge design and infrastructure resilience.

Two major earthquakes. One interchange. A forensic engineering comparison of repeated earthquake-related structural failures.

02 — CASE STUDY

Newhall Pass Interchange

The Newhall Pass Interchange is located in Los Angeles County and connects Interstate 5 with State Route 14. The interchange contains multiple bridge structures, flyover ramps, and a truck bypass.

Portions of the interchange experienced significant structural failures during both the 1971 San Fernando earthquake and the 1994 Northridge earthquake, making the site an important forensic engineering case study.

03 — 1971 SAN FERNANDO EARTHQUAKE

The First Major Failure

The 1971 San Fernando earthquake caused widespread structural and infrastructure damage throughout Southern California.

At the I-5/SR-14 interchange, bridge sections collapsed onto the freeway and truck bypass below. The damage demonstrated significant seismic vulnerabilities in the bridge system and became an important example of earthquake-related infrastructure failure.

04 — 1971 FAILURE INVESTIGATION

Possible Failure Mechanisms

The exact initiating mechanism of the 1971 collapse could not be established conclusively. Several possible mechanisms were identified in the technical literature and evaluated as part of the forensic investigation.

✓ Opening of a hinge joint caused by combined horizontal structural movement

✓ Loss of girder support

✓ Effects of vertical earthquake motion

✓ Possible bending failure of a supporting column

✓ Possible compression failure of a prestressed concrete box girder

✓ Pounding between adjacent bridge sections

✓ Insufficient seating and inadequate connections

Because the available evidence did not establish one definitive initiating mechanism, the 1971 failure remains an example of the uncertainty that can exist in forensic structural investigations.

05 — 1994 NORTHRIDGE EARTHQUAKE

The Interchange Fails Again

More than two decades after the San Fernando earthquake, the interchange experienced another major structural failure during the 1994 Northridge earthquake.

Portions of the SR-14/I-5 connector collapsed onto the main freeway and truck bypass, demonstrating that the reconstructed interchange remained vulnerable to severe seismic loading.

06 — STRUCTURAL DAMAGE

1994 Damage Investigation

The forensic investigation documented significant structural damage throughout the affected portion of the interchange.

Spans 1–3

Multiple bridge spans collapsed during the earthquake.

Pier 2

Pier 2 experienced severe crushing, shear distress, and nonductile behavior.

Pier 3

Pier 3 experienced significant structural damage and shear-related distress.

Superstructure

Significant horizontal movement of the bridge superstructure was observed.

In-Span Hinge

The hinge region experienced significant damage during the structural failure.

Connections

Connecting components, including equalizing bolts, experienced failure.

07 — 1994 FAILURE INVESTIGATION

Collapse Mechanism

Several possible collapse sequences were investigated for the 1994 failure. Unlike the 1971 event, the later forensic investigation provided stronger evidence about the likely initiating mechanism.

Hinge Unseating

One possibility considered whether loss of support at the in-span hinge initiated the collapse. The investigation found this mechanism unlikely to have been the initial failure.

Pier 2 Shear Failure

The investigation identified shear failure of Pier 2 as the most likely initiating structural failure, followed shortly by Pier 3 reaching its shear capacity.

Most likely 1994 initiating mechanism: shear failure of Pier 2.

08 — COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

1971 vs. 1994

1971 San Fernando

The initiating failure mechanism remained uncertain. Potential contributors included insufficient seating, hinge behavior, loss of support, pounding, column failure, and bridge configuration.

1994 Northridge

More detailed forensic evidence indicated that Pier 2 shear failure was the most likely initiating mechanism, followed by additional structural failures.

09 — FORENSIC ENGINEERING

Engineering Significance

This case study demonstrates how forensic engineers use observed damage, historical documentation, structural behavior, technical literature, and failure-mechanism evaluation to understand why structures fail.

Comparing two failures at the same interchange provides a unique opportunity to evaluate how reconstruction, seismic design practices, structural detailing, and retrofit strategies influenced structural performance.

10 — ENGINEERING LESSONS

Lessons for Seismic Bridge Design

✓ Provide sufficient bridge-seat width

✓ Provide reliable positive connections and restrainers

✓ Design bridge piers for adequate shear and flexural strength

✓ Provide sufficient ductility and deformation capacity

✓ Evaluate complete structural load paths

✓ Inspect hinges, bearings, connections, and supporting elements

✓ Evaluate pounding and relative movement between bridge sections

✓ Apply modern seismic retrofit requirements

✓ Use advanced structural analysis for irregular bridge systems

11 — RECOMMENDATIONS

Improving Infrastructure Resilience

Existing bridges should be evaluated using current seismic engineering requirements rather than relying only on the standards that were in place when the structures were originally constructed.

Pier shear strength, flexural strength, ductility, seat width, bearings, hinges, connections, and complete structural load paths should be carefully evaluated.

For irregular and complex bridge systems, advanced three-dimensional nonlinear structural analysis can provide a more complete understanding of expected earthquake behavior.

12 — CONCLUSION

Project Conclusion

The repeated failures of the I-5/SR-14 interchange demonstrate that reconstruction alone does not guarantee adequate earthquake performance.

Seismic resilience requires careful structural evaluation, ductile detailing, sufficient support lengths, reliable connections, effective inspection programs, and appropriate retrofit strategies.

Forensic engineering transforms structural failures into lessons that can improve the safety and resilience of future infrastructure.
