01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Why Project Delivery Methods Matter

Construction project delivery methods determine how responsibilities, risks, and contractual relationships are distributed among owners, designers, contractors, and construction managers.

These structural decisions influence project performance, coordination, dispute occurrence, risk allocation, and overall project success.

Delivery Method → Responsibility Allocation → Risk → Project Performance → Disputes

05 — LEGAL ANALYSIS

The Spearin Doctrine

The project examined the Spearin Doctrine in relation to Design-Bid-Build project delivery.

Under this principle, the owner bears responsibility for the accuracy and adequacy of owner-provided design documents, while contractors may receive protection from liability resulting from defective plans or specifications supplied by the owner.

06 — DESIGN-BUILD

Single-Point Responsibility

Design-Build combines design and construction responsibilities under one contracting entity.

This structure transfers greater design responsibility to the Design-Build contractor and can reduce disputes caused by coordination problems between separate designers and builders.

Design + Construction → Single Contracting Entity

07 — CMAR / CMGC

Collaborative Delivery

CMAR and CMGC structures emphasize collaboration, owner involvement, cost control, and shared management practices.

These approaches may incorporate Guaranteed Maximum Price arrangements to help manage financial risk and improve transparency.

09 — DESIGN DOCUMENT RISK

DBB Dispute Findings

The Türkiye case analysis demonstrated the importance of design-document quality in traditional Design-Bid-Build projects.

71%

Disputes associated with insufficient or inconsistent tender documents.

26%

Disputes associated with inaccurate, complex, or ambiguous design.

These findings demonstrate how poor design documentation can become a major source of construction disputes.

10 — SCHEDULE RISK

Delivery Method & Schedule Growth

The Ethiopia study identified schedule growth as an important concern in public-building projects.

Alternative delivery methods such as Design-Build and Construction Manager at Risk can improve coordination and help address time-related project risks.
