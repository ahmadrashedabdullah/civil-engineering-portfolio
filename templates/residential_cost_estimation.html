CONSTRUCTION ESTIMATION & BIM

Model-Based Residential Cost Estimation

Two-Story Residential Building | San Jose, California

A model-based construction estimating project integrating SketchUp, Revit, quantity takeoff, Excel, RSMeans, material pricing, labor costs, MEP components, and construction cost analysis for a two-story residential building.

Revit SketchUp BIM Quantity Takeoff Cost Estimation Excel RSMeans

01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project Objective

The objective of this project was to develop a comprehensive construction estimate for a two-story residential building using model-based quantity extraction and detailed construction cost analysis.

The project integrated digital modeling and estimating tools to connect building design information with quantities, material costs, labor costs, MEP costs, and other construction expenses.

BIM Model → Quantity Takeoff → Cost Data → Detailed Construction Estimate

05 — ARCHITECTURAL VISUALIZATION

SketchUp Model

SketchUp was used to develop and visualize the residential building based on the provided floor plan.

The model incorporated interior and architectural elements such as flooring, wall finishes, kitchen and bathroom fixtures, doors, windows, furniture, and accessories.

06 — BIM MODEL

Revit Model

Revit was used as the primary model-based quantity extraction tool.

Building components contained in the Revit model were organized into schedules so quantities could be extracted and transferred into the construction estimating workflow.

Revit → Schedules → Material Quantities → Construction Estimate

07 — QUANTITY TAKEOFF

Model-Based Quantity Extraction

Quantity takeoff was performed using Revit schedules and model information to determine quantities for major construction components.

Foundation

Concrete and reinforcement quantities.

Walls

Wall materials, plaster, paint, and associated finishes.

Flooring

Flooring materials and associated quantities.

Roofing

Roofing materials and surface-area quantities.

Doors & Windows

Frames, glazing, doors, and window quantities.

MEP

Electrical and plumbing fixtures and components.

08 — ESTIMATION METHODOLOGY

Developing the Cost Estimate

After quantities were extracted, construction cost information was researched and reviewed using multiple sources.

✓ Revit schedules were used for quantity takeoff

✓ RSMeans was used as a construction cost reference

✓ Local supplier information was reviewed

✓ Material prices were evaluated

✓ Labor rates and productivity were considered

✓ Cost assumptions were sanity-checked against additional sources

13 — ENGINEERING APPLICATION

Model-Based Construction Estimating

This project demonstrated how BIM tools can connect design information directly to construction estimating.

Using Revit schedules for quantity extraction reduced the need to manually calculate every building quantity and provided a structured workflow for transferring model information into an Excel-based cost estimate.

Combining digital models with construction cost databases and local market information provides a practical approach to early project cost planning.

14 — CONCLUSION

Project Conclusion

The project provided practical experience integrating BIM, quantity takeoff, construction cost databases, local market information, and spreadsheet-based estimating.

The final estimate demonstrated how building design decisions, material quantities, labor requirements, and local construction-market conditions combine to determine total project cost.

Final Estimated Construction Cost: $919,304 | $338 per sq ft
