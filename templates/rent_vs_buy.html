CONSTRUCTION OPERATIONS ANALYSIS

Equipment Rent vs. Buy Decision Tool

Monte Carlo Simulation • Python • Streamlit • Excel

An interactive construction decision-support tool developed to evaluate whether renting or purchasing a skid steer is more economical under uncertain equipment utilization, rental costs, ownership costs, and resale conditions.

Project Overview

Construction companies frequently need to decide whether equipment should be rented only when needed or purchased for repeated use across multiple projects.

This project evaluates the financial uncertainty associated with renting versus purchasing a wheeled skid steer. Rather than relying on one fixed cost estimate, the model uses Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate thousands of possible cost scenarios.

At what level of equipment utilization does buying become more economical than renting?

Decision Alternatives

Option 1 — Rent

Rent the skid steer only during the weeks that the equipment is required for construction projects.

Option 2 — Buy

Purchase the skid steer, use it across the same construction projects, account for ownership and operating costs, and recover part of the investment through resale at the end of the planning period.

Project Data

The model uses construction equipment utilization information and cost assumptions stored in an Excel workbook. The Streamlit application can load the workbook directly or allow the user to enter assumptions manually.

The project uses realistic synthetic construction data together with equipment cost assumptions to demonstrate how uncertainty affects the rent-versus-buy decision.

Interactive Streamlit Application

I used Streamlit to create an interactive graphical interface that allows users to perform the analysis without modifying the Python code.

The user can select a budget, upload an Excel workbook or use manual inputs, select the planning period, choose the number of Monte Carlo simulations, set a random seed, and modify equipment utilization.

Streamlit application interface showing the project budget, Excel workbook input, planning period, and Monte Carlo simulation settings.

Uncertain Inputs

Equipment utilization and costs cannot be predicted perfectly. The model therefore represents the major uncertain variables using triangular probability distributions defined by low, most likely, and high values.

Annual Utilization

Expected annual operating hours for the skid steer.

Weekly Rental Rate

Uncertainty in the weekly cost of renting the equipment.

Paid Rental Weeks

Number of weeks per year that rental equipment would be required.

Purchase Price

Variation in the initial cost of purchasing the skid steer.

Maintenance Cost

Annual maintenance represented as a percentage of the equipment purchase price.

Resale Value

Estimated terminal resale value at the end of the selected ownership period.

Fuel & Operating Cost

Hourly fuel and operating expenses applied during equipment operation.

Triangular probability inputs showing the low, most likely, and high assumptions used by the Monte Carlo simulation.

Monte Carlo Simulation Method

Monte Carlo simulation was used to evaluate uncertainty in the equipment decision. During each trial, the model randomly samples values from the input probability distributions and calculates the resulting rental and ownership costs.

This process is repeated thousands of times to create distributions of possible outcomes rather than relying on one deterministic estimate.

For the analysis shown here, the application ran 10,000 simulations. A random seed was also used so the same assumptions can reproduce the same simulation results.

Break-Even Analysis

The model evaluates how the economic decision changes as annual equipment utilization increases.

Average costs become approximately equal at 1.05× utilization, corresponding to approximately 8.44 paid rental weeks per year.

The 50% probability threshold occurs at approximately 8.46 paid rental weeks per year. Above this level, buying becomes cheaper in at least half of the simulated trials when annual rental duration is held fixed.

Break-even curve showing how the probability that buying is cheaper changes as annual equipment utilization increases.

Recommendation

Recommendation: RENT the equipment at the selected utilization level.

Under the selected assumptions, renting has the lower average total cost. The expected rental cost is approximately $67,715, compared with approximately $69,786 for purchasing.

Buying is cheaper in only 38.8% of the simulated scenarios at the selected utilization level.

However, the recommendation changes as equipment usage increases. When annual demand rises above approximately 8.46 paid rental weeks per year, purchasing becomes increasingly competitive.

Simulated Cost Distributions

Looking only at average costs can hide important uncertainty. The simulated distributions show the range of possible rental and ownership costs generated across the Monte Carlo trials.

The distributions also show the overlap between the two alternatives, helping explain why the preferred decision can change depending on the sampled conditions.
